## Foot & Ankle — quick anatomy & differentials

- **Chopart (transverse tarsal) joint** = talonavicular + calcaneocuboid joints. [Source: JAAOS 2016;24:379-389]
- **Talus** is the only tarsal bone with **no muscular attachments**; ~60% covered by articular cartilage, giving it a tenuous retrograde blood supply. [Source: AAOS Comprehensive Review 2, 2014]
- **Spring (calcaneonavicular) ligament** = superomedial (largest), medial plantar oblique, plantar inferior components; key static support of the medial arch. [Source: Mann's Surgery of the Foot and Ankle, 2014]
- **Most common accessory bone of the foot** = accessory navicular. [Source: JAAOS 2016;24:379-389]
- **Lateral ankle pain DDx:** lateral ankle instability, lateral talar process fracture, OLT, anterior calcaneal process fracture, 5th MT base fracture, peroneal pathology, subtalar instability, soft-tissue/bony impingement, tarsal coalition. [Source: AAOS Comprehensive Review 2, 2014]
- **Heel pain DDx:** plantar fasciitis, calcaneal stress fracture, Baxter's nerve entrapment, subcalcaneal bursitis, fat-pad atrophy, Sever disease, tumour. [Source: Mann's; AAOS Comprehensive Review 2, 2014]

## Hallux Valgus ⭐

- **Definition:** lateral deviation of the great toe with medial deviation of the first metatarsal. Sesamoids stay tethered to the adductor/transverse ligament, so the metatarsal head drifts medially off them and the crista erodes. [Source: Miller's 6th ed.; Mann's 2014]
- **Weight-bearing AP angles:** HVA ≤15°, IMA ≤9°, DMAA ≤15°, hallux valgus interphalangeus <10°. Also assess **joint congruency** (congruent vs incongruent/subluxated) and first-TMT arthrosis. [Source: AAOS Comprehensive Review 2, 2014; Mann's 2014]
- **Procedure algorithm (simplified):** IMA ≤13° & HVA ≤40° → **distal** metatarsal osteotomy (e.g. chevron); IMA >13° or HVA >40° → **proximal** osteotomy; first-TMT hypermobility/arthrosis or recurrence → **Lapidus** fusion; MTP arthritis/spasticity → **first-MTP fusion**; increased DMAA → add a redirectional distal osteotomy; increased HVI → add an **Akin**. [Source: Miller's 6th ed.]
- **Modified McBride distal soft-tissue procedure:** lateral release + medial eminence excision + medial capsular plication. The **fibular sesamoid is no longer routinely excised** (risk of hallux varus). [Source: Mann's 2014]
- **Most common complication of surgery = recurrence;** most common nerve injured = **dorsomedial cutaneous nerve** to the hallux. **Hallux varus** follows overcorrection/excess medial eminence resection/fibular sesamoidectomy; **transfer metatarsalgia** follows first-MT shortening or dorsiflexion malunion. [Source: Mann's 2014]
> Tested: 2025F, 2024S, 2022, 2021, 2020, 2019, 2018, 2016, 2014, 2012, 2008, 2007, 2004, Controversial

## Hallux Rigidus

- **Coughlin–Shurnas grading** combines dorsiflexion, radiographs and midrange pain (grade 0 → 4). Dorsal joint is affected **first**. [Source: JAAOS 2012;20:347-358]
- **Grade 1–2 without midrange pain → cheilectomy** (remove ~30% of the dorsal metatarsal head) ± **Moberg** (dorsal closing-wedge proximal phalanx osteotomy). **Grade 3–4 → first-MTP arthrodesis** (current standard). [Source: JAAOS 2012;20:347-358]
- **First-MTP fusion position:** 10–15° dorsiflexion relative to the floor, 10–15° valgus, neutral rotation; most stable construct = **dorsal plate + lag screw**. Most common complications = nonunion and metatarsalgia. **Arthroplasty is not recommended** (arthrodesis more reliable). [Source: JAAOS 2012;20:347-358]
> Tested: 2024S, 2021, 2016, 2014, 2009, 2008, 2004, Anki

## Bunionette, Lesser-Toe Deformities & Metatarsalgia

- **Bunionette (Coughlin):** Type 1 enlarged 5th MT head/exostosis → lateral condylectomy; Type 2 lateral bow of distal 5th MT (normal IMA) → distal chevron; Type 3 wide 4–5 IMA (>8°) → diaphyseal osteotomy. [Source: JAAOS 2007;15:300-307]
- **Deformity definitions:** mallet = DIP flexion; hammer = PIP flexion (MTP extended); claw = PIP+DIP flexion with MTP extension; curly = PIP+DIP flexion with MTP flexion/neutral. Assess flexible vs fixed with the **push-up test**. [Source: JAAOS 2011;19:505-514]
- **Flexible lesser-toe deformity → FDL transfer / soft-tissue balancing; fixed → PIP resection arthroplasty or fusion.** MTP instability (plantar-plate insufficiency) irreducible by soft tissue → **Weil (MT-shortening) osteotomy.** [Source: JAAOS 2011;19:505-514]
> Tested: 2025F, 2016, 2014, 2011, Controversial, Anki

## Morton's Interdigital Neuroma

- Perineural fibrosis of the common digital nerve, just **distal to the deep transverse metatarsal ligament**; most common in the **3rd webspace** (66%), then 2nd (32%); ~20% bilateral. [Source: Foot Ankle Surg 2018;24:92-98]
- **Mulder's sign** (painful click on forefoot compression); clinical diagnosis is the gold standard (US/MRI only for equivocal or multiple-neuroma cases). [Source: Foot Ankle Surg 2018;24:92-98]
- **First-line = orthotics/metatarsal pad ± steroid injection; refractory → resection** via dorsal approach, transecting the nerve proximally and burying the stump in intrinsic muscle. [Source: Foot Ankle Surg 2018;24:92-98]
> Tested: 2021, 2016, 2014, 2005, 2003

## Posterior Tibial Tendon Dysfunction / Adult Acquired Flatfoot ⭐

- **PTTD is the most common cause of adult-acquired flatfoot.** Findings: medial arch collapse, hindfoot valgus, forefoot abduction ("too-many-toes"), Achilles contracture, inability to perform a single-limb heel rise, inversion weakness. [Source: Miller's 6th ed.; AAOS Comprehensive Review 2, 2014]
- **Johnson–Strom staging:** I = tenosynovitis, no deformity (can heel-rise); II = flexible deformity (IIa/b/c by talonavicular uncoverage & forefoot abduction); III = **rigid** deformity; IV = deltoid failure with **lateral talar tilt / ankle arthritis**. [Source: Instr Course Lect 2015;64:441; Mann's 2014]
- **Treatment by stage:** I → immobilization then medial-post orthotic (operative = synovectomy); **II → FDL transfer to navicular + bony realignment** (MDCO for hindfoot valgus, lateral column lengthening for forefoot abduction, Cotton osteotomy or medial column fusion for fixed forefoot varus); **III → triple arthrodesis;** **IV → deltoid reconstruction + triple, or TTC fusion** if fixed. [Source: Instr Course Lect 2015;64:441; Mann's 2014; Miller's 6th ed.]
- **FDL is chosen** because it is adjacent, in-phase, same line of pull, and expendable; **MDCO** shifts the Achilles pull medially to restore inversion power. [Source: Mann's 2014]
> Tested: 2025F, 2024S, 2023, 2022, 2020, 2018, 2016, 2014, 2013, 2012, 2011, 2009, 2007, 2005, 2004, Controversial

## Cavovarus Foot & Charcot-Marie-Tooth ⭐

- **CMT muscle imbalance:** weak **tibialis anterior** and **peroneus brevis** with relatively strong **peroneus longus** and **tibialis posterior** → forefoot-driven plantarflexed first ray, hindfoot varus, claw toes, and equinus. [Source: JAAOS 2013;21:276-285]
- **Coleman block test** separates flexible from fixed hindfoot varus: heel + lateral foot on a block with the first ray free — if the hindfoot **corrects, it is forefoot-driven/flexible.** [Source: AAOS Comprehensive Review 2, 2014]
- **Flexible cavovarus (CMT) reconstruction:** dorsiflexion closing-wedge first-MT osteotomy + lateralizing (Dwyer) calcaneal osteotomy + PTT transfer, ± peroneus longus-to-brevis transfer and EDL/EHL (Jones) transfers. **Fixed hindfoot varus needs the calcaneal osteotomy;** flexible forefoot-driven varus can be treated by the first-MT osteotomy alone. [Source: JAAOS 2013;21:276-285]
> Tested: 2025F, 2024F, 2024S, 2021, 2020, 2018, 2016, 2015, 2014, 2013, 2012, 2011, 2010, 2009, 2008, 2007, 2003, Controversial

## Diabetic Foot

- **Triad:** sensory neuropathy (loss of protective sensation → repetitive trauma), autonomic neuropathy (dry, fissured skin), and PVD/ischaemia; deformity raises focal plantar pressure. [Source: JAAOS 2012;20:684-693]
- **Negative predictors of ulcer healing:** ABI <0.45, transcutaneous O₂ <30 mmHg, albumin <3.0 g/dL, total lymphocyte count <1,500/mm³. [Source: Orthobullets]
- **Foot ulceration is the single most significant risk factor for subsequent amputation.** Wagner grades ulcers 0–5 (0 = at-risk foot, 3 = deep abscess/osteomyelitis, 4–5 = gangrene). [Source: JAAOS 2012;20:684-693]
> Tested: 2021, 2018, 2016, 2015, 2014, 2012, 2009, 2003, Anki

## Charcot Arthropathy

- **Most common cause = diabetic neuropathy;** theories are neurotraumatic (loss of protective sensation) and neurovascular (autonomic-driven hyperaemia and bone resorption). Acute Charcot = **hot, swollen foot with bounding pulses** — erythema that **resolves with elevation** (unlike cellulitis). [Source: JAAOS 2009;17:562-571]
- **Eichenholtz stages:** 0 (normal x-ray, inflamed) → 1 fragmentation → 2 coalescence → 3 reconstruction. **Brodsky anatomic pattern:** Type 1 = tarsometatarsal (most common, rocker-bottom), Type 2 = subtalar/Chopart, Type 3 = ankle. [Source: JAAOS 2009;17:562-571]
- No single imaging test distinguishes Charcot from osteomyelitis; **bone scan followed by WBC-labelled scan** is most useful (sens 93–100%, spec ~80%). [Source: JAAOS 2009;17:562-571]
> Tested: 2014, 2011, 2009, 2006, 2005

## Acute & Chronic Achilles Tendon Rupture ⭐

- **Blood supply:** posterior tibial artery is the **main** supply (proximal >7 cm and distal <4 cm from insertion); peroneal artery supplies the 4–7 cm midsection **watershed**. Best open approach is **posteromedial** (protects sural nerve and the posterior-midline skin watershed). [Source: JAAOS 2017;25:23-31]
- **Functional rehab (early, protected weight-bearing) gives re-rupture rates comparable to surgery** and lower than cast immobilization; surgery's only reproducible advantages are earlier return to work and ~14% greater plantarflexion strength, at the cost of wound complications. [Source: JAAOS 2017;25:23-31]
- **Chronic = >4 weeks.** Reconstruct by post-debridement gap: <2 cm end-to-end; 2–5 cm V-Y ± FHL; 5–10 cm FHL transfer + turndown/graft; >10 cm FHL + allograft. **FHL is preferred** (strong, in-phase, closest axis of pull, adjacent, adds vascularity). **Matles test** (prone knee flexion) gauges resting tension. [Source: Foot Ankle Clin 2017;22:715-734; JAAOS 2018;26:753-763]
> Tested: 2025F, 2024F, 2024S, 2023, 2022, 2021, 2020, 2019, 2018, 2017, 2016, 2014, 2013, 2012, 2011, 2010, 2009, 2008, 2007, 2006, 2004, 2003, Anki

## Peroneal Tendon Injuries ⭐

- **Anatomy:** shared sheath begins ~4 cm above the fibular tip; **brevis is anterior/medial to longus** behind the fibula. **Superior peroneal retinaculum (SPR) is the primary restraint** to subluxation; dislocation mechanism = forceful **dorsiflexion + eversion.** [Source: JAAOS 2009;17:306-317]
- **Brevis tears** occur in the distal 3 cm over the fibular edge. Manage by CSA: <50% → debride, repair, tubularize; >50% → excise & tenodese to the intact tendon; both tendons torn → FHL transfer or allograft. [Source: JAAOS 2009;17:306-317]
- Radiographic clues: 5th-MT base avulsion (brevis), os peroneum fracture (longus), fibular fleck (SPR). **Correct hindfoot varus (Dwyer)** to prevent recurrence. [Source: JAAOS 2009;17:306-317]
> Tested: 2025F, 2024F, 2024S, 2022, 2015, 2014, 2009, 2008, 2005, 2003

## Chronic Lateral Ankle Instability ⭐

- **ATFL is the weakest lateral ligament and the primary restraint to inversion in plantarflexion.** [Source: JAAOS 2008;16:608-615; JAAOS 2018;26:223-230]
- **First-line surgery = anatomic repair (Broström) with the Gould modification** (extensor retinaculum reinforcement). Anatomic **reconstruction** (auto/allograft) is preferred for poor tissue, failed prior repair, high BMI, or generalized laxity. Non-anatomic tenodeses (Watson-Jones/Evans/Chrisman-Snook) are largely historical (they stiffen the subtalar joint). [Source: JAAOS 2018;26:223-230]
- **Always identify and correct hindfoot varus** (Coleman block: fixed → Dwyer/lateralizing calcaneal osteotomy; flexible → dorsiflexion first-MT osteotomy) to avoid failure. Look for associated OLT, peroneal tears, impingement. [Source: AAOS Comprehensive Review 2, 2014]
> Tested: 2025F, 2024S, 2021, 2015, 2014, 2012, 2009, 2006, 2003, Controversial

## Osteochondral Lesions of the Talus (OLT)

- **Medial lesions** are more common, atraumatic, larger/deeper and posterior; **lateral lesions** are traumatic, smaller/shallower and anterior. [Source: AAOS Comprehensive Review 2, 2014]
- Staging systems: **Berndt–Harty** (radiograph), **Ferkel** (CT), **Hepple** (MRI), Ferkel–Cheng (arthroscopic). [Source: Foot Ankle Int 2016;37:1023-1034]
- **Algorithm:** lesion <1.5 cm² → **microfracture** (gold standard for small lesions); >1.5 cm², cystic, or shoulder lesions → cartilage regeneration (ACI/MACI) or replacement (OAT/allograft); intact cartilage cap → retrograde drilling. [Source: Foot Ankle Orthop 2018]
> Tested: 2024F, 2023, 2020, 2015, 2013, 2010, 2009, 2008, Controversial

## Ankle Arthritis, Arthrodesis & Total Ankle Arthroplasty

- **~70% of ankle arthritis is post-traumatic.** Options: arthroscopic debridement (anterior impingement only), supramalleolar osteotomy (asymmetric varus/valgus with ≥50% joint preserved), distraction arthroplasty, arthrodesis, arthroplasty. [Source: AAOS Comprehensive Review 2, 2014; JAAOS 2016;24:e29-e38]
- **Ankle fusion position:** neutral dorsiflexion, **5° hindfoot valgus, 5–10° external rotation.** Main long-term cost is accelerated **subtalar** (most common) then talonavicular arthritis. [Source: Miller's 6th ed.; JAAOS 2016;24:e29-e38]
- **TAA contraindications:** active infection, insensate foot, severe deformity, **Charcot**, talar osteonecrosis, poor soft tissues, neuromuscular disease, osteopenia. Most common intra-op complication = **medial malleolus fracture;** leading revision cause = **aseptic loosening/subsidence.** [Source: JAAOS 2016;24:e29-e38; JAAOS 2015;23:272-282]
> Tested: 2025F, 2024F, 2021, 2019, 2011, 2007, 2006

## Plantar Fasciitis & Baxter's Nerve

- **Central band** (thickest) arises from the medial calcaneal tubercle. Classic **first-step morning pain;** positive **Windlass test**, negative tarsal-tunnel signs. **>90% resolve** with 3–6 months of stretching, orthoses/heel cups and night splints. [Source: Foot Ankle Orthop 2020;5(1):1-11]
- **Corticosteroid injection** gives only temporary relief and risks fat-pad atrophy and **fascial rupture.** Refractory cases → gastrocnemius recession or partial plantar fasciotomy. [Source: Foot Ankle Orthop 2020;5(1):1-11]
- **Baxter's nerve** = first branch of the **lateral plantar nerve;** entrapped between the deep fascia of abductor hallucis and the medial margin of quadratus plantae — causes chronic heel pain that mimics plantar fasciitis. [Source: Mann's 2014]
> Tested: 2025F, 2021, 2018, 2016, 2015, 2014, 2013, 2012, 2010, 2009, 2007, 2005, 2004, Anki

## Turf Toe & Sesamoid Injury ⭐

- **Hyperextension injury of the first MTP** damaging the plantar plate/capsule; mechanism = axial load on a plantarflexed foot with forced MTP dorsiflexion; risk factors = artificial turf, flexible shoes, game play. [Source: JBJS Reviews 2019;7(8):e7]
- Assess with the **dorsoplantar drawer;** radiographic red flags = proximal **sesamoid migration >3 mm**, sesamoid fracture/diastasis. Anderson grades I–III. [Source: JBJS Reviews 2019;7(8):e7]
- **Most Grade I–III are treated non-operatively** (stiff-sole shoe/boot). Surgery for large capsular avulsion, sesamoid diastasis/retraction, vertical instability, traumatic hallux valgus, or failed non-op care. [Source: JBJS Reviews 2019;7(8):e7]
> Tested: 2025F, 2024F, 2024S, 2022, 2021, 2019, 2018, 2016, 2014, 2008, 2004, Controversial

## Ankle Fracture & Syndesmosis ⭐

- **Danis–Weber** (fibula relative to syndesmosis): A below, B at, C above. **Lauge–Hansen** pairs foot position + talar force (SAD→WeberA vertical medial mall; SER→WeberB; PAB/PER→WeberC). [Source: CORR 2015;473:3323; JAAOS 2019;27:50-59]
- **Normal mortise:** medial clear space ≤4 mm, tibiofibular clear space <6 mm, tibiofibular overlap >6 mm (AP); talocrural angle 83°±4°. **Tibiofibular clear space is the most reliable syndesmosis measure** (position-independent). [Source: JAAOS 2015;23:510-518]
- **Operative indications:** displaced bi-/tri-malleolar fractures, lateral malleolus with incompetent deltoid (bimalleolar-equivalent), displaced medial malleolus, syndesmotic disruption, posterior malleolus >25%. **Fixing a posterior malleolus that captures the PITFL restores more syndesmotic stability (~70% vs ~40%) than a syndesmotic screw**, and suture-button fixation lowers malreduction/removal vs screws. Syndesmotic malreduction on post-op CT is as high as **~52%.** [Source: JAAOS 2013;21:32-40; AJSM 2019;47:2764; JAAOS 2015;23:510-518]
> Tested: 2025F, 2024F, 2023, 2022, 2021, 2020, 2019, 2018, 2016, 2015, 2014, 2010, 2009, 2008, 2007, 2006, 2005, 2004

## Talus Fracture ⭐

- **Overall most common talus fracture = lateral process ("snowboarder's").** **Talar neck** is the most common *major* body-region fracture (~50%), from hyperdorsiflexion. [Source: Rockwood & Green 8th ed.; Foot Ankle Surg 2018;24:282-290]
- **Blood supply:** artery of the tarsal canal (posterior tibial) is dominant to the body; deltoid branch supplies the medial third; dorsalis pedis supplies the head; tarsal sinus artery (peroneal). **Preserve the deltoid, inferior neck and sinus tarsi.** [Source: Rockwood & Green 8th ed.]
- **Hawkins classification & osteonecrosis risk:** I (nondisplaced, 0–~6%) → II (subtalar subluxation, ~16–21%) → III (subtalar + tibiotalar dislocation, ~39–45%) → IV (+ talonavicular). Only Hawkins I is treated non-operatively (confirm on CT). **Hawkins sign** (subchondral lucency at 6–8 wk) indicates preserved vascularity. [Source: Curr Rev Musculoskelet Med 2018;11:456-474]
- ORIF via **combined anteromedial + anterolateral** approaches; watch for **dorsal/medial comminution → varus & extension malunion.** [Source: Rockwood & Green 8th ed.; JOT 2015;29:385-392]
> Tested: 2024S, 2022, 2018, 2017, 2016, 2014, 2013, 2011, 2010, 2008, 2007, 2004, 2003, Controversial

## Calcaneus Fracture ⭐

- Assess **Böhler angle** (normal 20–40°, decreases with fracture) and the **critical angle of Gissane** (normal 120–145°, increases). Look for **associated lumbar-spine, pilon, plateau, contralateral** fractures. The **superomedial ("constant") fragment** stays attached to the sustentaculum. [Source: Rockwood & Green 8th ed.]
- **Essex-Lopresti:** tongue-type vs joint-depression. **Sanders (coronal CT)** grades the posterior facet I–IV by fracture lines; **Sanders IV** does poorly and is often primarily fused. [Source: Rockwood & Green 8th ed.]
- **Buckley RCT:** better ORIF results in **women, non-Workers'-Comp, younger (<29), Böhler >15°, lighter workload, simple pattern;** heavy-labour males on Workers' Comp did no better with surgery. The **wrinkle test** signals soft tissues are ready. Extensile lateral approach carries high wound-complication risk (smoking, diabetes, open) — **sinus-tarsi/percutaneous approaches reduce this.** [Source: JBJS Am 2002;84:1733-44; JAAOS 2015;23:399-407]
> Tested: 2025F, 2024S, 2023, 2022, 2021, 2020, 2019, 2018, 2014, 2011, 2010, 2009, 2008, 2007, 2006, 2005, 2004, 2003

## Navicular Fracture & Subtalar Dislocation ⭐

- **Navicular blood supply** is a central watershed (dorsalis pedis dorsally, plantar arteries) predisposing to **stress fracture** at the "N-spot"; **Sangeorzan** classifies body fractures I–III. Operative for joint incongruity >2 mm, medial-column shortening >3 mm, or irreducible/open injuries. Tuberosity avulsions can cause PTT insufficiency → flatfoot. [Source: JAAOS 2016;24:379-389]
- **Subtalar dislocation** = simultaneous talocalcaneal + talonavicular dislocation; **medial (~85%)** from inversion (talar head prominent dorsolaterally). **Block to reduction:** medial dislocation — talonavicular capsule/extensor tendons/EDB; **lateral dislocation — tibialis posterior tendon** (worse prognosis, higher energy). Reduce promptly, CT to check for intra-articular fragments. [Source: Rockwood & Green 8th ed.]
> Tested: 2024S, 2023, 2022, 2020, 2018, 2016, 2015, 2013, 2012, 2011, 2010, 2009, 2008, 2007, 2005, 2004, 2003, Controversial

## Lisfranc Injury

- **Up to 20% are missed initially;** untreated → post-traumatic arthritis and arch collapse. The **base of the second metatarsal recessed into the cuneiform mortise is the keystone** of the arch; the Lisfranc ligament runs from the medial cuneiform to the second MT base. [Source: JAAOS 2017;25:469-479]
- Look for the **"fleck sign,"** first–second MT diastasis, and loss of alignment on weight-bearing/stress views or CT. Unstable/displaced injuries → **anatomic ORIF or primary arthrodesis** (primary fusion favoured for purely ligamentous injuries). [Source: JAAOS 2017;25:469-479]
> Tested: 2025F, 2020, 2019, 2018, 2014, 2007, 2006, 2004, Controversial

## Fifth Metatarsal & Jones Fractures

- **Zones:** tuberosity avulsion (Zone 1) vs **metaphyseal–diaphyseal junction ("Jones," Zone 2)** vs proximal diaphyseal stress (Zone 3). The Zone 2/3 watershed predisposes to nonunion. [Source: Rockwood & Green 8th ed.]
- **True Jones fractures** in athletes are often fixed with an **intramedullary screw** for faster, more reliable union; tuberosity avulsions usually heal with symptomatic care. [Source: Rockwood & Green 8th ed.]
> Tested: 2018, 2016, 2014, 2003

## Tarsal Coalition & Tarsal Tunnel

- **Tarsal coalition** (talocalcaneal or calcaneonavicular) presents as a rigid, painful flatfoot with **peroneal spasm** in adolescence; calcaneonavicular seen on oblique foot film, talocalcaneal via CT. Resect (with interposition) if symptomatic and small; fuse if extensive or arthritic. [Source: Miller's 6th ed.]
- **Tarsal tunnel syndrome** = posterior tibial nerve compression behind the medial malleolus (positive Tinel); rule out a space-occupying lesion before release. [Source: Miller's 6th ed.]
> Tested: 2025F, 2015, 2010, 2009

## Ankle Arthroscopy — Portals & Nerves at Risk

- **Anteromedial** (medial to tibialis anterior) — risk **saphenous nerve/vein.** **Anterolateral** (lateral to peroneus tertius) — risk **superficial peroneal nerve** (intermediate dorsal cutaneous branch, the most commonly injured). **Posterolateral** (lateral to Achilles at fibular tip) — risk **sural nerve/small saphenous vein.** [Source: JAAOS 2008;16:635-646]
> Tested: 2025F (anatomy/portal recall)

## Common Peroneal Nerve Palsy

- **Most common compressive neuropathy of the lower limb;** compressed at the **fibular neck** → **foot drop / steppage gait** and, if untreated, **equinovarus.** [Source: JAAOS 2016;24:1-10]
- **Delay EMG/NCV 2–6 weeks** so Wallerian degeneration (fibrillations) is complete; earlier studies underestimate injury. [Source: JAAOS 2016;24:1-10]
- Treat compression non-operatively first (activity modification, AFO, physio); decompress if no recovery by ~3 months or progressive loss; **PTT transfer** for established, non-recovering drop foot. [Source: JAAOS 2016;24:1-10]
> Tested: 2010, 2008
