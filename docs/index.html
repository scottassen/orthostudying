How this was built

Source

The national consensus recall workbook, sheets Fall 2025, 2024-Fall, 2024-Spring, 2023, 2022, 2021, 2020, 2019, 2018 and 2017 — ten sittings, because 2024 ran in both spring and fall.

For 2020–2025, 1,328 stems were extracted and 81 were duplicates from the trailing repeat blocks several sheets carry, leaving 1,247. The 2017–2019 sheets added 504 rows, of which 22 were duplicates, 3 were blank and 9 were too vague to assign to any topic (recalled as a bare instruction to pick the true statement, with no clinical content left in them). Total: 1,717 questions, all read and classified. None are reproduced here.

Classification

Each stem was assigned to exactly one topic. Matching ran on the stem only in the first pass — when the full cell was matched, answer options drove the result, and every patella fracture question landed in Elbow because “tension band” appeared among its distractors.

Regional fractures sit with their region: distal radius under Hand, radial head under Elbow, ankle and hindfoot under Foot & Ankle. Trauma keeps polytrauma, open fractures, pelvis, hip, femur and tibia.

This edition

The first edition sorted and ranked the 2020–2025 topics. The second rebuilt every one of the twelve subspecialties as deeper reviews — the highest-frequency topic in each section written as its own small research synthesis rather than a summary.

This one extends the read back to 2017, adding the 2019, 2018 and 2017 sittings. The 2020–2025 classification is untouched: each card already carried its own per-year breakdown, so the earlier counts were preserved exactly and the new years added on top. Because the counts now span ten sittings rather than seven, the band thresholds and the high-yield cutoff were rescaled by the same factor — the filter is now 7× or more, and the bands break at 4, 7, 10 and 14.

One caveat on method. The 2017–2019 stems were classified for this edition, not by the pass that produced the original 2020–2025 mapping, so the two halves were assigned by different hands against the same 318 topics. Borderline stems — a coronoid approach question that could sit under Elbow or under Surgical approaches, say — may fall differently either side of 2020. Treat the counts as a guide to emphasis, not a precise tally.

One caveat worth holding onto. These are recalled questions, written from memory by candidates after the fact — not official examination material. Stems are often incomplete or paraphrased, and some recalls contradict one another. Treat the frequency counts as a reliable guide to emphasis, and the teaching points as a study framework to verify against your primary sources — not as a reproduction of the exam.
