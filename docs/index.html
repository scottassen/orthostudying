How this was built

Source

The national consensus recall workbook, sheets Fall 2025, 2024-Fall, 2024-Spring, 2023, 2022, 2021 and 2020 — seven sittings, because 2024 ran in both spring and fall.

1,328 stems were extracted; 81 were duplicates from the trailing repeat blocks several sheets carry. That left 1,247 questions, all of which were read and classified. None are reproduced here.

Classification

Each stem was assigned to exactly one topic. Matching ran on the stem only in the first pass — when the full cell was matched, answer options drove the result, and every patella fracture question landed in Elbow because “tension band” appeared among its distractors.

Regional fractures sit with their region: distal radius under Hand, radial head under Elbow, ankle and hindfoot under Foot & Ankle. Trauma keeps polytrauma, open fractures, pelvis, hip, femur and tibia.

This edition

The first edition sorted and ranked the topics. This one rebuilt every one of the twelve subspecialties as deeper reviews — the highest-frequency topic in each section written as its own small research synthesis rather than a summary.

Per-topic question counts, bands and sitting years are carried forward unchanged, so the frequency ranking and the “5+ only” filter still reflect the original read of the recalls. Every card uses the same shape: a bold lead-in per point, then a nested list, and its own collapsible reference list.

One caveat worth holding onto. These are recalled questions, written from memory by candidates after the fact — not official examination material. Stems are often incomplete or paraphrased, and some recalls contradict one another. Treat the frequency counts as a reliable guide to emphasis, and the teaching points as a study framework to verify against your primary sources — not as a reproduction of the exam.
