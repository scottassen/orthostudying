BONE FRAGILITY & DENSITY
Osteogenesis imperfectaCOL1A1 / COL1A2 (type I collagen) in ~85–90%; recessive forms CRTAP, P3H1/LEPRE1, PPIB, SERPINF1Autosomal dominant (types I–V); recessive forms existSillence: I mild, blue sclerae, no deformity (quantitative defect); II perinatal lethal; III progressively deforming, most severe survivable, white/normal sclerae; IV moderate, normal sclerae; V hyperplastic callus, interosseous membrane calcification.Bisphosphonates increase BMD and reduce fracture rate but do NOT improve final height or definitively reduce scoliosis. Type III is the most severe survivable and is the answer to 'which type is most severely affected'. Basilar invagination, dentinogenesis imperfecta, hearing loss, wormian bones.
OsteopetrosisCLCN7 (AD, Albers-Schönberg); TCIRG1 (AR malignant infantile); CA2 / carbonic anhydrase II (AR with renal tubular acidosis)AD (benign adult) or AR (malignant infantile)Failure of osteoclastic resorption → dense, brittle bone. 'Bone-within-bone', 'rugger-jersey spine', 'Erlenmeyer flask' femora. Marrow failure, cranial nerve compression, blindness.Fractures are transverse and heal poorly. Expect drill and reamer breakage, thermal necrosis and iatrogenic fracture — use sharp new bits, copious irrigation. CA2 subtype has renal tubular acidosis and cerebral calcification. AR form treated with haematopoietic stem cell transplant.
PycnodysostosisCTSK (cathepsin K) — osteoclast collagenaseAutosomal recessiveShort stature, dense bone, acro-osteolysis of the distal phalanges, open fontanelles, obtuse mandibular angle, frequent fractures.Toulouse-Lautrec. Distinguish from osteopetrosis by the acro-osteolysis and short stature.
HypophosphatasiaALPL (tissue non-specific alkaline phosphatase)AR (severe forms) / AD (milder)LOW alkaline phosphatase — the biochemical mirror image of most bone disease. Rickets-like changes, premature loss of deciduous teeth, fractures, craniosynostosis.Low ALP is the diagnostic clue — everything else that looks like rickets has a HIGH ALP. Treated with asfotase alfa. Bisphosphonates are contraindicated.
Fibrodysplasia ossificans progressivaACVR1 / ALK2 (BMP type I receptor, activating)Autosomal dominant (usually sporadic)Progressive heterotopic ossification of muscle, tendon and fascia; congenital hallux valgus with a short great toe is present at birth and is the diagnostic clue before any ossification appears.Biopsy, injection and surgery all trigger catastrophic flares — do NOT biopsy. The malformed great toe in a child with soft-tissue swellings is the exam giveaway.
MelorheostosisLEMD3 (germline); somatic MAP2K1Usually sporadic'Dripping candle wax' sclerotic cortical hyperostosis in a sclerotomal distribution, soft-tissue contracture, pain.Distribution follows sclerotomes, not dermatomes.
Osteopoikilosis / Buschke-OllendorffLEMD3Autosomal dominantMultiple small round sclerotic foci in the metaphyses and epiphyses; asymptomatic; with connective-tissue naevi in Buschke-Ollendorff.The classic mimic of blastic metastases — recognise it and do NOT work up for cancer.
Camurati-Engelmann (progressive diaphyseal dysplasia)TGFB1Autosomal dominantSymmetric diaphyseal cortical thickening of long bones, limb pain, waddling gait, muscle wasting.Steroid-responsive pain.
Caffey disease (infantile cortical hyperostosis)COL1A1Autosomal dominantInfant with fever, irritability, soft-tissue swelling and cortical hyperostosis, classically of the MANDIBLE and clavicle; self-limiting.A key mimic of non-accidental injury and osteomyelitis in an infant.
COLLAGEN & CONNECTIVE TISSUE
Marfan syndromeFBN1 (fibrillin-1), chromosome 15 — increased TGF-β signallingAutosomal dominantTall, arachnodactyly, arm span > height, pectus, joint hypermobility, SUPERIOR lens dislocation (ectopia lentis), aortic root dilatation and dissection, mitral prolapse, spontaneous pneumothorax.Scoliosis in ~60% — earlier, more rigid, more progressive, and responds poorly to bracing. Dural ectasia (60–90%) causes CSF leak and complicates instrumentation. Protrusio acetabuli. Echocardiogram before any surgery.
Loeys-Dietz syndromeTGFBR1 / TGFBR2 (also SMAD3, TGFB2)Autosomal dominantMarfanoid habitus with hypertelorism, bifid uvula/cleft palate, arterial tortuosity and aneurysms that rupture at smaller diameters than in Marfan.Much higher rate of cervical spine instability than Marfan — image the neck. Vascular risk is greater.
Ehlers-Danlos, classicalCOL5A1 / COL5A2Autosomal dominantSkin hyperextensibility, atrophic 'cigarette-paper' scars, joint hypermobility, recurrent dislocation.Soft-tissue repairs and stabilisations have high failure rates — counsel accordingly.
Ehlers-Danlos, vascular (type IV)COL3A1 (type III collagen)Autosomal dominantThin translucent skin, easy bruising, spontaneous arterial, bowel and uterine rupture; characteristic facies.The dangerous subtype — elective surgery is hazardous and arteriography can be fatal. Must be distinguished from the hypermobile type.
Ehlers-Danlos, hypermobileLargely unknownAutosomal dominantGeneralised joint hypermobility (Beighton score), chronic pain, subluxations, minimal skin findings.By far the commonest type seen in clinic; management is physiotherapy and proprioceptive training, not surgery.
Stickler syndromeCOL2A1 (type I); COL11A1/COL11A2Autosomal dominantSevere myopia with retinal detachment, sensorineural hearing loss, midface hypoplasia, Pierre Robin sequence, early OA, joint hypermobility.Ophthalmology referral is mandatory — retinal detachment is preventable blindness.
HomocystinuriaCystathionine β-synthase (CBS)Autosomal recessiveMarfanoid habitus but with INFERIOR/nasal lens dislocation, developmental delay, thrombotic events, osteoporosis, scoliosis.The key Marfan mimic. Inheritance is RECESSIVE, lens dislocates DOWN, intelligence is impaired, and there is a thrombotic risk that changes perioperative management. Diagnose with urine sodium nitroprusside / plasma homocysteine.
Larsen syndromeFLNB (filamin B)Autosomal dominantMultiple congenital joint dislocations (knees, hips, elbows), flattened facies with depressed nasal bridge and hypertelorism, equinovarus feet, short spatulate thumbs.CERVICAL KYPHOSIS AND INSTABILITY — screen before any anaesthetic, as myelopathy and death have occurred.
Nail-patella syndrome (hereditary onycho-osteodysplasia)LMX1BAutosomal dominantAbsent/hypoplastic patellae, dystrophic nails, iliac horns (pathognomonic), elbow dysplasia with radial head dislocation, nephropathy.Iliac horns on the AP pelvis are pathognomonic. Screen renal function.
NEUROMUSCULAR
Duchenne muscular dystrophyDystrophin (DMD gene) — absent proteinX-linked recessiveBoys, onset 3–6 yr: waddling gait, toe walking, calf pseudohypertrophy, Gowers sign, proximal → distal weakness, CK elevated 10–100×. Loss of ambulation ~10–12 yr; death from cardiorespiratory failure.Fuse the spine EARLY — at ~20–30° while FVC is still >30–40%. Steroids prolong ambulation and delay scoliosis. Anaesthesia: avoid succinylcholine and volatile agents (hyperkalaemic arrest, rhabdomyolysis) — use TIVA.
Becker muscular dystrophyDystrophin — reduced but presentX-linked recessiveSame phenotype as DMD but later onset and slower progression; ambulation preserved past 16 years.Allelic with DMD; the in-frame vs out-of-frame deletion distinction.
Myotonic dystrophy (type 1)DMPK — CTG trinucleotide repeat expansionAutosomal dominant, with anticipationDistal weakness, myotonia (delayed relaxation — inability to release a handshake), frontal balding, cataracts, cardiac conduction block, clubfoot.Distal rather than proximal weakness — the opposite of most dystrophies. Cardiac conduction disease demands preoperative ECG.
Charcot-Marie-Tooth 1A (HMSN I)PMP22 duplication, chromosome 17Autosomal dominantBilateral CAVOVARUS feet, hammer toes, 'stork leg'/inverted champagne bottle calves, intrinsic wasting, absent reflexes, slow nerve conduction velocities (demyelinating).The commonest inherited neuropathy and the commonest cause of bilateral cavovarus. Weak tibialis anterior and peroneus brevis with relatively strong peroneus longus and posterior tibialis drives the deformity.
CMT X-linkedGJB1 (connexin 32)X-linkedSimilar phenotype; males more severely affected than females.Second commonest CMT genotype.
Friedreich ataxiaFXN (frataxin) — GAA trinucleotide repeat expansionAutosomal recessiveOnset 8–15 yr: progressive ataxia and dysarthria, loss of proprioception and vibration, areflexia WITH extensor plantars, bilateral cavovarus feet, scoliosis, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, diabetes.Cerebellar signs + cavovarus + areflexia is pathognomonic. Cardiomyopathy is the usual cause of death — echocardiogram before surgery. The scoliosis behaves more like idiopathic than neuromuscular.
Spinal muscular atrophySMN1 deletion (SMN2 copy number modifies severity)Autosomal recessiveAnterior horn cell degeneration: symmetric proximal weakness with tongue fasciculations, areflexia, preserved intelligence and sensation. Type I (Werdnig-Hoffmann) infantile and severe; type III (Kugelberg-Welander) milder.Nusinersen and gene therapy have transformed the natural history — modern patients survive longer and present with scoliosis and hip subluxation. Very high rate of progressive scoliosis requiring fusion to the pelvis.
Rett syndromeMECP2X-linked dominant (lethal in males)Girls with normal early development then regression, loss of purposeful hand use with stereotypic hand-wringing, seizures, acquired microcephaly.Rapidly progressive neuromuscular scoliosis — often requires fusion; bracing is ineffective.
Emery-Dreifuss muscular dystrophyEMD (emerin); LMNAX-linked recessive (LMNA form is AD)Early contractures (elbow, Achilles, cervical) preceding weakness, humeroperoneal weakness, cardiac conduction defects requiring pacemaker.Contractures BEFORE weakness is the distinguishing pattern; the cardiac risk is life-threatening and out of proportion to the weakness.
Congenital insensitivity to pain (HSAN IV)NTRK1Autosomal recessiveAbsent pain and temperature sensation, anhidrosis, self-mutilation, Charcot joints, painless fractures, osteomyelitis.A key differential for suspected non-accidental injury and for a child with multiple painless fractures.

HAEMATOLOGIC
