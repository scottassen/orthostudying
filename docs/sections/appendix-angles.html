SHOULDER & ELBOW
Critical shoulder angle (CSA)On a true AP: angle between a line from the superior to inferior glenoid rim and a line from the inferior glenoid to the most lateral acromion.30–35°>35° → degenerative rotator cuff tear (increased superiorly directed shear). <30° → glenohumeral osteoarthritis (increased joint compression). One of the more predictive shoulder measurements.
Acromiohumeral interval (AHI)Shortest distance between the undersurface of the acromion and the humeral head on a true AP.7–14 mm<7 mm indicates a large/massive chronic cuff tear with superior migration, and is one of the classic markers of an irreparable tear. <5 mm suggests static superior escape.
Glenoid versionOn axial CT: angle between the glenoid face and a line perpendicular to the scapular body axis (Friedman line).−7° to +2° (mean ~ −5°, i.e. slightly retroverted)Retroversion >15–20° (Walch B2/B3) markedly increases the risk of glenoid component loosening and posterior instability after anatomic TSA — consider augmented components, eccentric reaming or reverse arthroplasty.
Glenoid track0.83 × D, where D is the glenoid diameter, minus the width of any anterior glenoid bone loss.Hill-Sachs interval within the track = on-trackOff-track (Hill-Sachs interval exceeds the remaining track) predicts engagement and Bankart failure → add remplissage or perform a Latarjet. Combine with the glenoid bone loss %: <13–15% and on-track → Bankart; >20–25% → Latarjet.
Baumann angleOn a true AP of the elbow: angle between the humeral shaft axis and a line along the lateral condylar physis.70–75° (compare with the contralateral side — side-to-side difference of >5° is significant)Assesses coronal alignment after supracondylar fracture reduction; an increased Baumann angle indicates varus malreduction, which produces cubitus varus.
Anterior humeral lineA line drawn down the anterior humeral cortex on a true lateral.Should pass through the middle third of the capitellumPassing anterior to the capitellum (or through the anterior third) indicates an extension-type supracondylar fracture — the most useful single sign in a subtle injury. Note it may normally pass through the anterior third in children under 4.
Radiocapitellar lineA line down the axis of the radial neck.Must pass through the capitellum on EVERY viewDisruption indicates radial head dislocation — the key to not missing a Monteggia lesion. Scrutinise it on every paediatric forearm or elbow film.
Carrying angleClinical angle between the humeral and forearm axes with the elbow extended and supinated.5–15° valgus (greater in women)Cubitus varus (gunstock deformity) after supracondylar malunion; cubitus valgus after lateral condyle nonunion, which causes a tardy ulnar nerve palsy.
WRIST & HAND
Radial inclinationOn PA: angle between a line along the distal radial articular surface (radial styloid tip to the ulnar corner of the lunate facet) and a line perpendicular to the radial shaft.22° (range 19–29°)Loss indicates radial shortening/impaction. Restore in distal radius fixation.
Radial height (length)On PA: distance between two lines perpendicular to the radial shaft, one through the radial styloid tip and one through the ulnar corner of the distal radius.11–13 mmAcceptable post-reduction loss is <5 mm (equivalently, ulnar variance within 2–3 mm of the contralateral side).
Volar tiltOn lateral: angle between a line along the distal radial articular surface and a line perpendicular to the radial shaft.11° volar (range 2–20°)Acceptable is within 10–15° of neutral (i.e. <10–20° dorsal angulation) depending on the patient's demand. Dorsal angulation shifts load to the ulna and alters carpal kinematics (adaptive midcarpal instability).
Ulnar varianceOn a standardised zero-rotation PA: the difference in level between the distal ulnar articular surface and the ulnar corner of the distal radius.0 ± 2 mm (neutral)Positive variance → ulnar impaction syndrome and higher rates of TFCC central tears. Negative variance → association with Kienböck disease. Variance increases with pronation and grip — technique matters.
Scapholunate angleOn a true lateral: angle between the scaphoid axis and the lunate axis.30–60°>70° = DISI (dorsal intercalated segment instability, from scapholunate ligament injury). <30° = VISI (volar, from lunotriquetral injury). Also check the radiolunate angle (>15° abnormal).
Scapholunate gap (Terry Thomas sign)On PA, ideally a clenched-fist view.<3 mm (compare with the contralateral wrist)>3 mm suggests scapholunate dissociation. Accompanied by the cortical ring sign (foreshortened, flexed scaphoid) and disruption of Gilula's arcs.
Gilula's arcsThree smooth arcs on the PA: (I) proximal carpal row proximal surfaces, (II) proximal row distal surfaces, (III) proximal surfaces of the capitate and hamate.All three smooth and uninterruptedAny step or break indicates a carpal instability or perilunate injury — the fastest screen for a missed perilunate dislocation, which is missed in up to 25% of cases.
Carpal height ratioCarpal height (third metacarpal base to distal radius along the third metacarpal axis) ÷ length of the third metacarpal.0.54 ± 0.03Reduced in carpal collapse — SLAC/SNAC wrist and Kienböck disease.
Hallux valgus angle (HVA)Weightbearing AP foot: angle between the longitudinal axes of the first metatarsal and the proximal phalanx.<15°Mild 15–20°, moderate 20–40°, severe >40°. Guides procedure selection alongside the IMA.
HIP & PELVIS
Lateral centre-edge angle of Wiberg (LCEA)On AP pelvis: angle between a vertical line through the femoral head centre and a line from the head centre to the lateral edge of the sourcil.>25° normal20–25° borderline dysplasia; <20° dysplastic; >40° overcoverage/pincer (coxa profunda). The single most-used measure of lateral acetabular coverage.
Anterior centre-edge angle (ACEA, Lequesne)On the false profile view: angle between a vertical line through the head centre and a line to the anterior acetabular edge.>20–25°<20° indicates deficient ANTERIOR coverage — cannot be assessed on the AP, and a key part of PAO planning.
Tönnis angle (acetabular index of the weightbearing zone)Angle between a horizontal line and a line from the medial to the lateral edge of the sourcil.0–10°>10° = dysplasia/instability; <0° (negative) = overcoverage/pincer. Complements the LCEA.
Acetabular index (Hilgenreiner, paediatric)Angle between Hilgenreiner's line and a line from the triradiate cartilage to the lateral acetabular margin.<30° at birth; <25° at 6 months; <20° at 2 yearsThe standard measure of acetabular dysplasia in the young child; falls with normal development.
Migration percentage (Reimers index)Percentage of the femoral head width lying lateral to Perkin's line.<10% normal>30% = subluxation requiring intervention; >50% = significant displacement (VDRO + pelvic osteotomy + soft-tissue release); >100% = dislocation. The backbone of cerebral palsy hip surveillance.
Alpha angleOn a 45° Dunn or radial/oblique-axial MRI: angle between the femoral neck axis and a line from the head centre to the point where the head-neck contour exceeds the radius of the best-fit circle.<55°>55–60° indicates CAM morphology. Note that cam morphology is present in a substantial proportion of asymptomatic athletes — it is not a diagnosis on its own; treat the patient, not the angle.
Femoral neck-shaft angleAngle between the femoral neck axis and the femoral shaft axis on AP.125–135°<120° coxa vara; >140° coxa valga. Coxa valga with excessive anteversion is the femoral deformity of DDH and cerebral palsy.
Hilgenreiner-epiphyseal (HE) angleAngle between Hilgenreiner's line and the capital femoral physis.<25°>60° predicts PROGRESSION in congenital coxa vara and is the indication for valgus osteotomy (target HE <38°). 45–60° is the watch zone.
Shenton's lineA continuous arc from the inferior border of the femoral neck to the superior border of the obturator foramen.Smooth and unbrokenDisruption indicates hip subluxation/dislocation (DDH), a femoral neck fracture, or SCFE. A quick screen on every AP pelvis.
Klein's lineA line along the superior border of the femoral neck on AP (and frog-leg).Should intersect the lateral part of the capital femoral epiphysisFailure to intersect (or a smaller intersected segment than the contralateral side — the modified Klein / Trethowan sign) indicates SCFE. The modified version is substantially more sensitive.
Southwick slip angleOn frog-leg lateral: difference between the epiphyseal-shaft angle of the affected and normal sides.Mild <30°, moderate 30–50°, severe >50°Grades SCFE severity; guides consideration of realignment versus in-situ fixation.
Posterior sloping angle (PSA)On frog-leg lateral of the contralateral hip: angle between the physeal line and a line perpendicular to the femoral shaft.<12°>14° predicts a contralateral slip and is an indication for prophylactic pinning. The modified Oxford bone age score is the other validated predictor.
Femoral anteversionCT or clinical (Craig/trochanteric prominence) test.Adult 10–15° (newborn ~30–40°, decreasing with growth)Excessive anteversion → in-toeing, patellofemoral instability, and is the femoral deformity in DDH and CP. >50° in a child is the threshold often used for derotational osteotomy if symptomatic.
Acetabular anteversion (component, post-THA)Cross-table lateral or CT.Lewinnek 'safe zone': inclination 40 ± 10°, anteversion 15 ± 10°The Lewinnek zone does not reliably prevent dislocation — a majority of dislocating cups in modern series lie within it. Spinopelvic mobility (change in sacral slope <10° between standing and sitting = a stiff spine) matters more.
KNEE
Mechanical axis / hip-knee-ankle angleLine from the femoral head centre to the ankle centre on a long-leg standing film.Passes just medial to the centre of the knee; HKA ~1.0–1.5° varusThe target of osteotomy and alignment philosophy in arthroplasty. The anatomic axis differs from the mechanical axis by 5–7° of valgus.
Lateral distal femoral angle (LDFA, mechanical)Lateral angle between the femoral mechanical axis and the distal femoral joint line.87° (85–90°)Localises a coronal deformity to the femur. Used with the MPTA to decide where to osteotomise.
Medial proximal tibial angle (MPTA)Medial angle between the tibial mechanical axis and the proximal tibial joint line.87° (85–90°)<85° indicates a tibia-based varus deformity — the correct site for a high tibial osteotomy. Osteotomising the wrong bone creates joint line obliquity.
Posterior tibial slopeOn a true lateral: angle between the tibial plateau and a line perpendicular to the tibial shaft axis.7–10°Increased slope (>12°) is an independent risk factor for ACL injury and graft failure, and increases anterior tibial translation. Slope-reducing osteotomy is considered in revision ACL with slope >12–14°. Increasing slope opens the flexion gap in TKA.
Insall-Salvati ratioPatellar tendon length ÷ patellar bone length on a 30° flexion lateral.0.8–1.2>1.2 = patella alta (instability, later engagement in the trochlea); <0.8 = patella baja/infera (post-surgical, arthrofibrosis). Unreliable after tibial tubercle osteotomy — use Caton-Deschamps.
Caton-Deschamps indexDistance from the inferior patellar articular surface to the anterosuperior tibia ÷ patellar articular surface length.0.6–1.3Preferred over Insall-Salvati after tubercle osteotomy because it references the articular surface, not the tendon insertion. >1.3 = alta.
TT-TG distance (tibial tubercle–trochlear groove)On axial CT or MRI: mediolateral distance between the deepest point of the trochlear groove and the centre of the tibial tubercle, referenced to the posterior condylar line.<15 mm normal>20 mm is abnormal and is a threshold for tibial tubercle medialisation. MRI values run ~2–4 mm smaller than CT — do not mix modalities. The TT-PCL distance isolates tubercle lateralisation from trochlear dysplasia.
Sulcus angle / congruence angleOn a Merchant view: sulcus angle between the medial and lateral trochlear facets; congruence angle between the sulcus bisector and a line to the patellar apex.Sulcus ~138°; congruence −6° ± 11A shallow (increased) sulcus angle >145° indicates trochlear dysplasia — the strongest anatomic risk factor for recurrent patellar dislocation. A positive congruence angle indicates lateral subluxation.
Patellar tilt angleAngle between the lateral patellar facet and the posterior condylar line on axial imaging.<10–20°Increased tilt indicates lateral retinacular tightness and correlates with instability and lateral facet overload.
Blumensaat's lineThe line of the intercondylar notch roof on a true lateral.The lower pole of the patella should lie at the level of Blumensaat's line at 30° flexionA quick screen for patella alta, and the reference for ACL femoral tunnel and Schöttle's point for MPFL reconstruction.
FOOT & ANKLE
Böhler angleOn lateral: angle between a line from the anterior process to the highest point of the posterior facet, and a line from that point to the superior tuberosity.20–40°Decreased (or negative) with a depressed intra-articular calcaneal fracture. A Böhler angle of 0–14° identified the subgroup that benefited most from operative treatment in the Canadian Buckley trial.
Critical angle of GissaneAngle between the lateral downslope of the posterior facet and the anterior process, on lateral.120–145°Increased with a depressed posterior facet — the complementary measurement to Böhler.
Meary's angle (talo-first metatarsal)Angle between the long axis of the talus and the long axis of the first metatarsal on a weightbearing lateral.0° (colinear); ±4° acceptableApex plantar (dorsally convex) = CAVUS. Apex dorsal = PLANUS. The single best measure of arch height, and the reference for cavovarus and flatfoot reconstruction.
Calcaneal pitchAngle between the inferior calcaneal surface and the floor on a weightbearing lateral.17–32°<17° = pes planus; >30° = pes cavus. Read alongside Meary's angle.
Talocalcaneal (Kite) angleAP: angle between the talar and calcaneal longitudinal axes.AP 20–40°; lateral 25–50°Decreased (<20°, with the axes parallel) in CLUBFOOT — parallelism of the talus and calcaneus on both views is the hallmark. Increased in planovalgus.
Hallux valgus & intermetatarsal anglesWeightbearing AP: HVA between the first metatarsal and proximal phalanx; IMA between the first and second metatarsals.HVA <15°; IMA <9°IMA drives procedure choice: <13° → distal osteotomy (chevron); >13–15° → proximal osteotomy or Lapidus. Also measure the DMAA (<10°) — an increased DMAA with a congruent joint is characteristic of juvenile hallux valgus and must be corrected or recurrence follows.
Talocrural angleMortise view: angle between the intermalleolar line and a line parallel to the tibial plafond.83° ± 4 (compare with the contralateral side)A change indicates fibular shortening or malrotation after ankle fracture fixation.
Tibiofibular clear spaceMortise or AP, measured 1 cm above the plafond: distance between the lateral border of the posterior tibial malleolus and the medial border of the fibula.<6 mmThe most reliable radiographic measure of syndesmotic integrity, because it is least affected by limb rotation.
Tibiofibular overlapMeasured 1 cm above the plafond.>6 mm on the AP; >1 mm on the mortiseThe differing thresholds between the AP and mortise view are a classic exam trap. Less reliable than clear space because it varies with rotation.
Medial clear spaceMortise: space between the lateral border of the medial malleolus and the medial border of the talus, at the level of the talar dome.≤4 mm, and equal to the superior clear spaceWidening indicates deltoid incompetence and, in an isolated distal fibular fracture, converts a stable injury into an unstable one requiring fixation.
SPINE
Cobb angleAngle between lines along the endplates of the most tilted end vertebrae (or their perpendiculars).<10° is not scoliosis by definitionBrace 25–40° in a skeletally immature patient; operate >45–50°. Interobserver error is ±5°, so a change of <5° is not progression.
Rib-vertebral angle difference (Mehta angle)Difference between the convex and concave rib-vertebral angles at the apex, in infantile scoliosis.<20°RVAD >20°, or a phase II rib (rib head overlapping the vertebral body), predicts PROGRESSION in infantile idiopathic scoliosis; <20° predicts spontaneous resolution.
Pelvic incidence (PI)Angle between a line perpendicular to the sacral endplate at its midpoint and a line from that midpoint to the femoral head centre.50° ± 10 — a FIXED, morphologic parameterPI = PT + SS. Does not change with position or surgery. Determines the lumbar lordosis a given patient requires.
PI − LL mismatchPelvic incidence minus lumbar lordosis.Within ±10°The parameter most strongly associated with disability in adult spinal deformity and the primary realignment target (Schwab/SRS).
Pelvic tilt (PT)Angle between the vertical and a line from the sacral endplate midpoint to the femoral head centre.<20°A POSITIONAL parameter — rising PT indicates pelvic retroversion compensating for sagittal imbalance. A high PT means the patient is spending energy standing upright.
Sagittal vertical axis (SVA)Horizontal offset of a plumb line from the C7 vertebral body centre to the posterosuperior corner of S1.<5 cm>5 cm correlates with pain and disability. Read alongside PT — a patient can have a normal SVA while retroverting the pelvis heavily to achieve it.
Atlanto-dens interval (ADI)Lateral: distance between the posterior aspect of the anterior C1 arch and the anterior dens.Adults <3 mm; children <5 mm>3 mm (adult) indicates transverse ligament incompetence. In rheumatoid arthritis, >10 mm implies complete ligamentous disruption.
Posterior atlanto-dens interval (PADI) / space available for the cordDistance from the posterior dens to the anterior aspect of the posterior C1 arch.>14 mmPADI <14 mm predicts neurologic compromise in rheumatoid arthritis and is a stronger surgical indication than the anterior ADI — the more useful of the two measurements.
Power's ratio(Basion to posterior arch of C1) ÷ (opisthion to anterior arch of C1).<1.0>1.0 indicates ANTERIOR atlanto-occipital dissociation. Insensitive to posterior/vertical dissociation.
Basion-dens and basion-axial intervals (BDI / BAI — Harris' rule of 12s)BDI: basion to the dens tip. BAI: basion to the posterior axial line.Both <12 mmScreen for occipitocervical dissociation. The condylar-C1 interval (CCI) >2 mm on CT is the most sensitive measure. Traction is absolutely contraindicated in this injury.
Torg-Pavlov ratioSagittal canal diameter ÷ vertebral body diameter on a cervical lateral.>0.8<0.8 suggests developmental cervical stenosis, historically used in return-to-play decisions after cervical cord neurapraxia. Poor positive predictive value in athletes with large vertebral bodies — MRI assessment of functional cord reserve is now preferred.
Metaphyseal-diaphyseal (Drennan) angleAngle between a line across the proximal tibial metaphyseal beaks and a line perpendicular to the tibial shaft.<11° likely physiologic>16° suggests BLOUNT DISEASE; 11–16° is indeterminate and requires follow-up. The key discriminator from physiologic bowing.
Slip percentage (Meyerding) and slip angleMeyerding: percentage translation of the superior on the inferior vertebra. Slip angle: lumbosacral kyphosis at the slip.Meyerding I <25%, II 25–50%, III 50–75%, IV 75–100%, V spondyloptosisThe SLIP ANGLE predicts progression and clinical outcome better than the percentage slip. High-grade slips (>50%) are a surgical indication.
